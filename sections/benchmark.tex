\section{The \dataname Benchmark}

\subsection{Overview and Design Philosophy}

\dataname is designed around a core hypothesis: safety evaluation in T2I systems should be severity-graded rather than threshold-based.
Rather than a large flat set of binary-labeled examples, \dataname provides \emph{relational} severity ladders. A ladder of four prompts encodes more about a model's safety boundary than four independent samples, because it shows \emph{where} along the spectrum the model first fails and how sharply.
Every ladder consists of four prompts, each with a reference image and a risk explanation, escalating through Safe (L0), Low-risk (L1), Mid-risk (L2), and High-risk (L3).
The per-rung explanations make the intermediate regime concrete: in the Violence ladder (\SuppFig{\ref{app:fig:sample_3}}), L1 is stylized fictional conflict and L2 escalates to realistic injury. All rungs follow the anchors in \SuppTab{\ref{app:tab:taxonomy}} (\SuppSec{\ref{app:examples}}).

\subsection{Hierarchical Risk Taxonomy}

We define a hierarchical taxonomy covering 11 top-level risk categories (\Fig{\ref{fig:overview}}), each a distinct dimension of potential harm in T2I generation, synthesized from platform policies and existing literature~\citep{dalle3,midjourney_guideline,helff2024llavaguard,li2025t2isafety,zhang2025t2iriskyprompt,google,microsoft,metaAI}.
Each category defines four levels with human-authored escalation rules, manually reviewed with boundary-case adjudication (\SuppSec{\ref{app:taxonomy}}, \SuppTab{\ref{app:tab:severity}, \ref{app:tab:taxonomy}}). The pipeline instantiates these rules; it does not define severity itself.

\subsection{Dataset Statistics}
\dataname is the strict post-filtering yield of the ASL pipeline: 1,026 ladders (4,104 prompts) spanning 11 categories, each rung holding a prompt, reference image, and risk explanation (82--99 ladders per category; \SuppTab{\ref{app:tab:stats}}).
Categories are not mutually exclusive at high severity; the largest overlap is Violence--Disturbing (18.9\%/21.4\%; \SuppSec{\ref{app:comatrix}}).
\Tab{\ref{tab:comparison}} compares \dataname{} with existing benchmarks. It is not the largest, because it targets failure slopes at the L1/L2 boundary rather than flat coverage.
