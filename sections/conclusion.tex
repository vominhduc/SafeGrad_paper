Unlike existing benchmarks that treat safety as a binary property, SafeGrad probes the gradated safety boundary through a hierarchical risk taxonomy of 11 categories and 4 severity levels, constructed via an automated adversarial severity interpolation pipeline that ensures semantic coherence and visual consistency across the risk gradient. Using SafeGrad, we conducted a comprehensive evaluation of 6 T2I models, 9 defense methods, 5 safety filters, and 5 adversarial attacks, yielding 11 key insights into the strengths and limitations of current T2I safety mechanisms.

We hope SafeGrad, together with its publicly released construction pipeline, serves as a foundation for developing more robust, boundary-aware T2I safety systems that protect against the full spectrum of risk rather than its most obvious extremes.

T2I safety evaluation has been measuring the wrong target: existing mechanisms certify safety at the most explicit extreme while the real deployment risk lives in the L1--L2 regime, where prompts appear benign yet generate harmful content.
\dataname (1,026 severity ladders across 11 categories and four levels, each with prompts, reference images, and per-rung risk explanations) makes this gap measurable through graded relational evaluation rather than binary labels.
Our results show the gap is universal across model families, defense paradigms, and filters, and structurally exploitable via adversarial attacks.
