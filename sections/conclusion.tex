\section{Conclusion}
\looseness=-1
T2I safety evaluation has been measuring the wrong target: existing mechanisms certify safety at the explicit extreme, while deployment risk lives in the L1--L2 regime, where prompts appear benign yet images do not.
\dataname (1,026 ladders, 11 categories) makes this gap measurable through graded, relational evaluation rather than binary labels.
Across 6 models, 9 defenses, and 6 filters, we find that the gap is universal and that attacks exploit it. A ladder-tuned filter closes it to 2.2--2.4\% bypass under prompt-aware inference (matched zero-shot anchors 37.5--65.3\%), including on fresh generations from all six models, so the gap is trainable rather than intrinsic.
Closing it requires severity-calibrated responses across the full spectrum, not L3 suppression alone; we release \dataname{} and ASL for that purpose.
