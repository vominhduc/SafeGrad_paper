\section{Conclusion}

T2I safety evaluation has been measuring the wrong target: existing mechanisms certify safety at the most explicit extreme while the real deployment risk lives in the L1--L2 regime, where prompts appear benign yet images do not.
\dataname (1,026 ladders $\times$ 4 rungs over 11 categories, with prompts, reference images, and per-rung risk explanations) makes this gap measurable through graded relational evaluation rather than binary labels.
The gap holds across every model, defense paradigm, and filter we tested (6 T2I models, 9 defenses on a common SD1.4 backbone, 5 filters), adversarial attacks exploit it structurally, and a ladder-tuned filter reduces the bypass rate to 2.2--2.4\% under prompt-aware inference (protocol-matched zero-shot anchors: 52.2--65.3\%), including on fresh generations from all six models: the gap is trainable rather than intrinsic. Closing it therefore requires severity-calibrated response across the full spectrum, not L3 suppression alone; we release \dataname and the ASL pipeline for that effort.
