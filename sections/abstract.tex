\begin{abstract}

Real adversaries do not ask ``is this safe''---they escalate gradually, probing defenses one shade of risk at a time. Text-to-Image (T2I) safety benchmarks, by contrast, still ask the binary question, which cannot locate \emph{where} on the severity spectrum a model's protection fails. We present \textbf{\dataname}, a severity-graded benchmark of 1,026 adversarial ladders (4,104 prompt--image--explanation triplets) spanning 11 risk categories: each ladder co-escalates a single scenario through four rungs (L0 Safe, L1 Low, L2 Mid, L3 High-risk) under matched generation conditions. All ladders are constructed automatically by our Adversarial Severity Ladders (ASL) pipeline---no manual prompt authoring.
Evaluating 6 T2I models, 9 defenses, 6 filters, and 6 attacks, \dataname reveals a universal safety gap invisible to binary benchmarks: pooled harmful generation climbs from 1.7\% at L0 to 65.8\% at L3, with the steepest jump ($+28.0$ pp) at the L1$\to$L2 boundary; binary-calibrated defenses suppress relatively more harm at L1 than at L3, leaving the largest absolute residual harm at L2--L3, while all six standard filters stay near-flat across severity, missing the L1--L2 band and much of L3 content.
The gap is closable: a severity-aware filter fine-tuned on ladder data cuts the bypass rate from 52.2\% to 2.2\% against the protocol-matched zero-shot backbone (88.0\% unguided), outperforms all six baselines, and \red{transfers across model families and to fresh generations from every model} under prompt-aware inference---evidence the gap is trainable rather than intrinsic.
\red{\dataname{} and the ASL pipeline will be released for boundary-aware safety research at \url{https://vmdlab.github.io/safegrad/}.}

\end{abstract}
