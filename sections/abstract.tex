\begin{abstract}

Real adversaries do not ask ``is this safe,'' they escalate gradually. Text-to-Image (T2I) safety benchmarks still answer the former: binary safe/unsafe evaluation cannot locate \emph{where} on the severity spectrum a model's defenses fail. We present \textbf{\dataname}, a benchmark of 1,026 severity ladders (4,104 prompt--image--explanation triplets) spanning 11 risk categories, each co-escalating a single scenario through four rungs (Safe, Low-risk, Mid-risk, High-risk) under matched generation conditions, all constructed automatically by our Adversarial Severity Ladders (ASL) pipeline without manual prompt authoring.
Across 6 T2I models, 9 defenses, 5 filters, and 6 attacks, \dataname reveals a universal safety gap: binary-calibrated mechanisms protect L1 relatively more than L3 yet leave the largest absolute residual harm at L2--L3, a pattern invisible to binary benchmarks because standard filters show near-flat detection across severity levels. All absolute rates are measured under one policy-conditioned evaluator protocol (blind judging lowers absolute rates without changing any cross-method ordering).
A severity-aware filter fine-tuned on ladder data reduces the bypass rate from 52.2\% to 2.2\% against the protocol-matched zero-shot backbone (88.0\% when fully unguided), outperforms all five baselines, and transfers to fresh generations from every evaluated model under prompt-aware inference: the diagnosed gap is trainable rather than intrinsic to the models.
\dataname and the ASL pipeline will be publicly released to support boundary-aware safety research.

\end{abstract}
