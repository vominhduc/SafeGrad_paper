\begin{abstract}

Real adversaries do not ask ``is this safe?''; they escalate gradually, probing defenses one shade of risk at a time. Text-to-Image (T2I) safety benchmarks still ask the binary question, which cannot locate \emph{where} on the severity spectrum a model's protection fails. We present \textbf{\dataname}, a severity-graded benchmark of 1,026 adversarial ladders (4,104 prompt--image--explanation triplets) spanning 11 risk categories. Each ladder escalates a single scenario through four rungs (L0 Safe, L1 Low, L2 Mid, L3 High-risk) under matched generation conditions, and all ladders are built automatically by our Adversarial Severity Ladders (ASL) pipeline, with no manual prompt authoring.
We evaluate 6 T2I models, 9 defenses, 6 filters, and 6 attacks. \dataname reveals a universal safety gap that binary benchmarks cannot see: pooled harmful generation climbs from 1.7\% at L0 to 65.8\% at L3, with the steepest jump ($+28.0$ pp) at the L1$\to$L2 boundary. Binary-calibrated defenses suppress relatively more harm at L1 than at L3, so the largest absolute residual harm sits at L2--L3, and all six standard filters stay near-flat across severity, missing the L1--L2 band and much of L3.
The gap can be closed: a severity-aware filter fine-tuned on ladder data cuts the bypass rate from 52.2\% to 2.2\% against the protocol-matched zero-shot backbone (88.0\% unguided), outperforms all six baselines, and transfers across model families and to fresh generations from every model under prompt-aware inference. The gap is trainable rather than intrinsic.
\dataname{} and the ASL pipeline will be released for boundary-aware safety research at \url{https://vmdlab.github.io/safegrad/}.

\end{abstract}
