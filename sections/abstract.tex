\begin{abstract}

Real adversaries do not ask ``is this safe''---they escalate gradually, probing a model's defenses one shade of risk at a time. Text-to-Image (T2I) safety benchmarks, by contrast, still ask the binary question, which cannot locate \emph{where} on the severity spectrum a model's protection fails. We present \textbf{\dataname}, a severity-graded benchmark of 1,026 adversarial ladders (4,104 prompt--image--explanation triplets) spanning 11 risk categories: each ladder co-escalates a single scenario through four rungs (Safe, Low-risk, Mid-risk, High-risk) under matched generation conditions, and all ladders are built automatically by our Adversarial Severity Ladders (ASL) pipeline, without manual prompt authoring.
Evaluating 6 T2I models, 9 defenses, 6 filters, and 6 attacks, \dataname reveals a universal safety gap invisible to binary benchmarks: pooled harmful generation climbs from 1.7\% at the safe rung to 65.8\% at the high-risk rung, with the steepest jump ($+28.0$ pp) at the low-to-mid boundary; binary-calibrated defenses suppress relatively more harm at L1 than at L3, leaving the largest absolute residual harm at L2--L3, while all six standard filters stay near-flat across severity levels, missing the L1--L2 band and, for most, a large share of L3 content.
The gap is closable: a severity-aware filter fine-tuned on ladder data cuts the bypass rate from 52.2\% to 2.2\% against the protocol-matched zero-shot backbone (88.0\% when fully unguided), outperforms all six baselines, and transfers across model families and to fresh generations from every evaluated model under prompt-aware inference---evidence that the gap is trainable rather than intrinsic.
\dataname and the ASL pipeline will be publicly released to support boundary-aware safety research.

\end{abstract}
