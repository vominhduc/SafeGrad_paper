\section{Introduction}

\noindent\fcolorbox{red!55!black}{red!4}{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}{\small\textbf{\textcolor{red!55!black}{Content warning:}} potentially offensive examples follow.}}

\vspace{0.3em}
\par\noindent
Text-to-Image (T2I) generative models such as Stable Diffusion 3~\citep{SD3}, DALL-E 3~\citep{dalle3}, and Midjourney~\citep{midjourney} have transformed content creation, yet their susceptibility to harmful or graphic content has become the primary bottleneck for safe deployment~\citep{bird2023typology,yang2023sneakyprompt}.
Deployed guardrails typically pair a prompt-level text filter with a post-generation visual checker, and both are calibrated on explicit violations: they stop direct requests for nudity or graphic violence reliably, but they struggle with adversarial, nuanced, or metaphor-based inputs~\citep{zhang2025adversarial}.
What such defenses cannot answer is where along the harm spectrum the boundary actually breaks.

Current T2I safety benchmarks~\citep{yang2023sneakyprompt,qu2023unsafe,schramowski2023safe,li2025t2isafety,zhang2025t2iriskyprompt} predominantly test binary ``safe/unsafe'' robustness, which cannot locate such boundary failures: real adversaries escalate gradually, and different applications demand graded rather than binary judgments.
We identify a critical \emph{safety gap}: the regime where the text filter passes the prompt and the visual checker passes the image, yet the image already falls in a nontrivial severity band.
Because threshold-based defenses are calibrated at the explicit extreme, they are weakest precisely at this low-to-mid-risk boundary; the slip is particularly hazardous for child-oriented platforms or enterprise safe-search, where failing to separate ``safe'' from ``low-to-mid-risk'' content directly exposes users to harmful material.

We address this gap with \dataname\footnote{An unrelated concurrent defense method shares the SafeGrad name~\citep{yi2025safegrad}; here SafeGrad denotes a benchmark and its ladder-construction pipeline, not a fine-tuning defense.}, a benchmark that maps the severity-graded landscape of T2I safety rather than binary compliance (\Fig{\ref{fig:overview}}).
We synthesize the usage policies of leading T2I platforms into a principled taxonomy of 11 risk categories with pre-defined escalation rules, and use our Adversarial Severity Ladders (ASL) pipeline to build 1,026 ladders escalated through four rungs (Safe L0 to High-risk L3), each carrying prompts, reference images, and risk explanations constructed without manual prompt authoring.

Evaluated on \dataname, the current T2I safety ecosystem shows the gap everywhere we look.
\textbf{(1) T2I models:} 6 open-source models leak harmful content even at the safe rung, with harm rates ordered by generative capability.
\textbf{(2) Defenses:} 9 methods spanning inference-guided, model-editing, and fine-tuning paradigms all suppress failures proportionally more at L1 than at L3, steepening the severity gradient rather than closing it.
\textbf{(3) Safety filters:} all 5 filters track severity poorly; none reliably detects L1--L2 content.
\textbf{(4) Adversarial attacks:} 6 attack families exploit this asymmetry directly, with query-based and multimodal attacks (ASR $\geq$ 63.4\%) outperforming white-box gradient methods against the best-defended configurations.
\textbf{(5) Remediation:} a severity-aware filter fine-tuned on ladder data recovers the boundary band that zero-shot filters miss, on benchmark reference images and on fresh generations from every evaluated model (Insight~6).
The seven resulting insights trace one arc: the gap is universal (Insight 1), even the safe rung leaks (Insight 2), neither defenses nor filters close it (Insights 3--5), attacks exploit it (Insight 7), and ladder data can close it (Insight 6).

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item We define a hierarchical safety taxonomy spanning 11 risk categories and 4 calibrated severity levels, providing a principled foundation for boundary-aware T2I safety evaluation.

\item We propose the automated Adversarial Severity Ladders (ASL) pipeline, which constructs semantically coherent, monotonically escalating prompt--image--explanation triplets without manual prompt authoring.

\item We present \dataname, a benchmark of 1,026 ladders (4,104 triplets) offering the most granular view of T2I safety boundaries to date.

\item We evaluate the T2I ecosystem end-to-end, yielding seven insights from a universal L1 blind spot invisible to binary benchmarks to a ladder-tuned filter that detects boundary content missed by zero-shot filters.
\end{itemize}
