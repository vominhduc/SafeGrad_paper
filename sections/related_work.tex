\section{Related work}
\textbf{T2I safety benchmarks.}
The landscape of T2I safety evaluation has evolved from simple NSFW detection toward complex, multi-dimensional taxonomies.
Early benchmarks like I2P~\citep{schramowski2023safe} (4,703 prompts, 7 categories) and Unsafe Diffusion~\citep{qu2023unsafe} (434 prompts) sourced real user prompts from Lexica to study NSFW generation.
SneakyPrompt~\citep{yang2023sneakyprompt} introduced 200 adversarially crafted prompts targeting pornographic and violent imagery.
Recent efforts~\citep{dai2024safesora,miao2024t2vsafetybench,li2025t2isafety,zhang2025t2iriskyprompt} expanded coverage to more risk dimensions.
Most related to our work, T2ISafety~\citep{li2025t2isafety} established a broad framework assessing fairness, toxicity, and privacy across $\sim$70K prompts; T2I-RiskyPrompt~\citep{zhang2025t2iriskyprompt} introduced a hierarchical taxonomy of 6 primary and 14 fine-grained categories with reasoning-based annotation.
Yet, all of these benchmarks treat safety as a binary property: a prompt is either safe or unsafe.
% \dataname departs from this paradigm in a fundamental way: rather than cataloging the breadth of unsafe content, we probe the \emph{depth} of the safety boundary through Adversarial Severity Ladders that map the incremental transition from benign to high-severity violations.
% This design enables us to quantify the safety gap -- the L1--L2 severity band where models pass binary safety filters yet produce visually harmful outputs -- a failure mode that is structurally invisible to prior benchmarks.
\dataname instead probes the \emph{depth} of the safety boundary via Adversarial Severity Ladders, enabling quantification of the L1--L2 safety gap that is structurally invisible to binary benchmarks.
\red{Although our evaluation matrix (models, defenses, filters, attacks) follows the protocol of contemporary work for baseline consistency, \dataname's relational, severity-graded structure yields findings that flat binary prompt sets cannot produce, e.g., the relative-suppression asymmetry at L1 in Insight~5, which requires paired, monotonic ladders to observe.}

\textbf{Defenses and filters.}
Internal defenses modify model weights~\citep{gandikota2023concept,gandikota2024unified} or retrain safety objectives~\citep{liu2024safetydpo,lu2024mace,chen2025trce}; external ones apply keyword lists~\citep{george}, text classifiers~\citep{li_NSFW_text_classifier,Detoxify,khader2024diffguard}, image-level detectors~\citep{laionAI,Chhabra,schramowski2023safe,Giphy}.
They all target L3-equivalent explicit content under a binary safe/unsafe protocol.
\dataname provides the first graded diagnostic across all four severity rungs, revealing that defenses effective at L3 remain vulnerable to low-to-mid-risk escalation.
