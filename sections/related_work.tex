\section{Related work}
\textbf{T2I safety benchmarks.}
The landscape of T2I safety evaluation has evolved from simple NSFW detection toward complex, multi-dimensional taxonomies.
Early benchmarks like I2P~\citep{schramowski2023safe} (4,703 prompts, 7 categories) and Unsafe Diffusion~\citep{qu2023unsafe} (434 prompts) sourced real user prompts from Lexica to study NSFW generation.
SneakyPrompt~\citep{yang2023sneakyprompt} introduced 200 adversarially crafted prompts targeting pornographic and violent imagery.
Recent efforts~\citep{dai2024safesora,miao2024t2vsafetybench,li2025t2isafety,zhang2025t2iriskyprompt} expanded coverage to more risk dimensions.
Most related to our work, T2ISafety~\citep{li2025t2isafety} established a broad framework assessing fairness, toxicity, and privacy across $\sim$70K prompts, and T2I-RiskyPrompt~\citep{zhang2025t2iriskyprompt} introduced a hierarchical taxonomy of 6 primary and 14 fine-grained categories with reasoning-based annotation.
Both, however, still assign each prompt a single, independently sampled safe/unsafe verdict: neither pairs multiple severity instances of the same underlying scenario under matched generation conditions, so neither can compute how a defense's harm suppression at one severity relates to its suppression at another for a fixed scenario.
This distinction is not cosmetic: it is precisely what Insight~3 (\Sec{\ref{sec:filter}}) requires.
Every defense we test achieves higher \emph{relative} HGR reduction at L1 than at L3 -- e.g., MACE reduces NSFW L1 HGR by $\rho_{L1}=84.6\%$ versus $\rho_{L3}=69.4\%$ at L3 -- yet absolute residual harm still peaks at L3, so a binary evaluation would report MACE as simply ``strong'' while missing that its protection is skewed toward the severity band least likely to be probed.
Observing this skew requires holding the underlying scenario fixed while varying severity (the same T2I model and seed across all four rungs of a ladder); a flat, independently sampled prompt set cannot hold scenario fixed this way, making the asymmetry structurally invisible to it.
\dataname's Adversarial Severity Ladders are designed specifically to enable this comparison, and our evaluation matrix (models, defenses, filters, attacks) otherwise follows the protocol of contemporary work for baseline consistency.

\textbf{Defenses and filters.}
Internal defenses modify model weights~\citep{gandikota2023concept,gandikota2024unified} or retrain safety objectives~\citep{liu2024safetydpo,lu2024mace,chen2025trce}; external ones apply keyword lists~\citep{george}, text classifiers~\citep{li_NSFW_text_classifier,Detoxify,khader2024diffguard}, image-level detectors~\citep{laionAI,Chhabra,schramowski2023safe,Giphy}.
They all target L3-equivalent explicit content under a binary safe/unsafe protocol.
\dataname provides the first graded diagnostic across all four severity rungs, revealing that defenses effective at L3 remain vulnerable to low-to-mid-risk escalation.
