\textbf{Evaluation scope.} T2I models are evaluated with safety checkers disabled (raw generative risk); defenses are tested only on SD1.4 because weight-modifying methods (UCE, RECE, MACE, TRCE) require architecture-specific reimplementation for DiT-based models; extending to newer architectures is an important direction for future work. Architecture-agnostic findings (relative HGR reduction patterns, the MACE vs.\ TRCE tradeoff) are expected to transfer; paradigm rankings may not.

\noindent
\textbf{Coverage.} Construction models are excluded from evaluation; Copyright and PID show lower HGR due to MLLM classifier limitations on fine-grained identity harms; severity definitions reflect a Western legal framework; prompts are English-only.
\red{Concretely, our cross-cultural analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:cultural}) shows that non-Western entities incur higher L3 HGR, so \dataname's absolute HGR values should be read within this scope (see Discussion); applying the taxonomy to other jurisdictions requires revising the severity definitions, which the ASL pipeline supports at low cost.}

\noindent
\textbf{Evaluator error.} MLLM ensemble evaluation introduces noise particularly at L1; errors may slightly underestimate the safety gap.
\red{A blinded human validation study (500 stratified pairs, three judges, 60\% of the budget at L1/L2) reaches $\kappa = 0.73$ agreement with the pipeline's severity labels, and a blinded evaluator ablation shifts HGR by under 2.4\% on macro average, bounding this error empirically.}


\section{Ethical Considerations}
The construction and release of a T2I safety benchmark necessarily involves generating and handling harmful content; we take several precautions to minimize ethical risk.

\noindent\textbf{Data access.}
Reference images at L2 and L3 severity are distributed only to verified safety researchers under a restricted access agreement; the Minors category carries additional restrictions consistent with legal requirements in most jurisdictions, and we will consult our legal office before public release.

\noindent\textbf{Dual-use risk.}
The ASL pipeline can generate adversarial prompts at scale and could be misused to craft attacks rather than evaluate defenses; we therefore release only the validated benchmark ladders and pipeline code under a research-only license that prohibits harmful-content generation outside safety evaluation.
All evaluated models and evaluators are used under their respective research licenses, and safety checkers are disabled solely as a controlled experimental condition, not for deployment.

\noindent\textbf{Personal data and offensive content.}
\dataname is entirely synthetic: all prompts are LLM-generated and all images T2I-generated. PID contains only simulated documents (fictitious passports, ID cards, credentials) and Public Figures only AI-generated depictions; the full dataset was manually spot-checked to confirm no real personally identifying information was inadvertently included.

\noindent\textbf{Responsible disclosure and artifacts.}
We will share evaluation results with the developers of all evaluated models and filters prior to public release, providing a 60-day window for remediation.
SafeGrad (benchmark ladders, prompts, risk explanations) and the ASL pipeline code will be released under a CC BY-NC 4.0 license, with L2--L3 reference images under a separate restricted-access agreement; a datasheet documenting composition, collection procedure, intended uses, and known limitations is included in the repository.
