\textbf{Evaluation scope.} T2I models are evaluated with safety checkers disabled (raw generative risk); defenses are tested only on SD1.4 because weight-modifying methods (UCE, RECE, MACE, TRCE) require architecture-specific reimplementation for DiT-based models -- extending to newer architectures is an important direction for future work. Architecture-agnostic findings (relative HGR reduction patterns, the MACE vs.\ TRCE tradeoff) are expected to transfer; paradigm rankings may not.

\noindent
\textbf{Coverage.} Construction models are excluded from evaluation; Copyright and PID show lower HGR due to MLLM classifier limitations on fine-grained identity harms; severity definitions reflect a Western legal framework; prompts are English-only.
\red{Concretely, our cross-cultural analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:cultural}) shows that non-Western entities incur higher L3 HGR ($+13.4$ pp for Public Figures, $+11.2$ pp for Copyright) than Western equivalents -- an imbalance inherited from the safety-alignment data underlying current models rather than from the benchmark design -- so \dataname's absolute HGR values should be read within that scope (see Discussion). Because jurisdictions differ in safety regulation, applying the taxonomy elsewhere requires adapting the severity definitions, which the ASL pipeline regenerates at low cost.}

\noindent
\textbf{Evaluator error.} MLLM ensemble evaluation introduces noise particularly at L1; errors may slightly underestimate the safety gap.
\red{A blinded human validation study (500 stratified pairs, three judges, 60\% of the budget at L1/L2) reaches $\kappa = 0.73$ agreement with the pipeline's severity labels, and a blinded evaluator ablation shifts HGR by under 2.4\% on macro average, bounding this error empirically.}


\section{Ethical Considerations}
The construction and release of a T2I safety benchmark necessarily involves generating and handling harmful content.
We take several precautions to minimize ethical risk.

\noindent\textbf{Data access.}
Reference images at L2 and L3 severity levels are distributed only to verified safety researchers under a restricted access agreement.
The Minors category is subject to additional restrictions beyond the general L2--L3 policy, consistent with legal requirements in most jurisdictions.
We will consult with our legal office before making \dataname publicly available.


\noindent\textbf{Dual-use risk.}
The ASL pipeline can generate adversarial prompts at scale and could be misused to craft attacks rather than evaluate defenses.
We therefore release only the validated benchmark ladders and the pipeline code under a research-only license that prohibits use for generating harmful content outside of safety evaluation contexts.
All existing T2I models and evaluators are used under their respective research licenses; safety checkers are disabled solely as a controlled experimental condition to measure raw generative risk, consistent with permitted research use and not intended for deployment.


\noindent\textbf{Personal data and offensive content.}
\dataname is entirely synthetic: all prompts are LLM-generated and all images are T2I-generated; no real personal data was collected from any individual.
The PID category contains only simulated documents (fictitious passports, ID cards, and credentials) and the Public Figures category uses only AI-generated depictions — no real photographs are reproduced.
The full dataset was manually spot-checked to confirm no real personally identifying information was inadvertently included.
The offensive and harmful visual content produced at L2--L3 is an intended property of the benchmark and is handled under the access restrictions described above.


\noindent\textbf{Responsible disclosure.}
We will share evaluation results with the developers of all evaluated models and filters prior to public release, providing a 60-day window for remediation.

\noindent\textbf{Artifacts.}
SafeGrad (benchmark ladders, prompts, and risk explanations) and the ASL pipeline code will be released under a CC BY-NC 4.0 license.
L2--L3 reference images are available under a separate restricted-access agreement as described above.
A datasheet documenting dataset composition, collection procedure, intended uses, and known limitations is included in the repository.
