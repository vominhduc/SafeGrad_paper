\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Evaluation scope.} T2I models are evaluated with checkers disabled (raw generative risk), and defenses are tested only on SD1.4 because weight editing needs architecture-specific DiT ports.

\noindent
\textbf{Coverage.} \looseness=-1 Construction models and closed commercial systems are excluded (weight access, reproducibility). Severity definitions are Western-centric, and prompts are English-only. Insight~5's claim about filters in general rests on the three generalist judges, since NSFW-targeted filters and single-category detectors (e.g., NudeNet~\citep{chhabra2020nudenet}) are out-of-domain on Legal and Social content. The human study uses three annotators; a larger panel and a fully blind harm rating are planned.

\noindent
\textbf{Evaluator error.} \looseness=-1 The evaluator is noisiest at L1 (human $\kappa = 0.73$; guidance-ablation shift $<2.4\%$). Because it sees sample-specific risk descriptions, absolute HGR figures are protocol-relative (at L3, 7.2\% blind vs.\ 76.4\% guided), and L0 rates are the noise floor. The label-stripping ablation retains the sample-specific descriptions, so description-cue bias is not fully excluded; an image-grounded audit is future work.
The ladder scorer and verifier are Qwen-family models, but the cross-family remediation run (InternVL2-8B: FBR 40.1\% $\to$ 1.3\%) shows that the transfer is not a same-family teacher artifact.
Finally, correlation within a ladder means the reported SEMs are understated; ladder-level resampling would widen them.
