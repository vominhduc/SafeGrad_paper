\subsection{The Safety Gap: A Universal Failure Mode}\label{sec:gap}

\Fig{\ref{fig:safety_gap}} shows the aggregated HGR gradient is far from uniform (image-level HGR, macro-averaged across the eleven categories): while mean HGR@L3 is 65.8\%, the L0$\rightarrow$L1 transition already produces a 21.1 percentage-point (pp) jump at the rung where filters are least calibrated.
The escalation steepens at L1$\rightarrow$L2 (mean $\Delta$HGR = 28.0 pp), instantiating the safety gap of the Introduction here: prompts benign to text filters produce visually harmful outputs that evade classifiers trained on explicit L3 content.

\textbf{Insight 1: Universal L1 blind spot.} \textit{Current T2I safety systems exhibit a systematic blind spot at the L1 boundary, universal across all evaluated models and invisible to binary safe/unsafe evaluation.}
\Tab{\ref{tab:delta}} confirms this universality (per-category SEM 0.8--1.9 pp across the eleven categories): the L1$\to$L2 transition is the steepest in the aggregate profile (28.0 vs.\ 21.1 pp at L0$\to$L1) and the peak transition for five of six models (HiDream's L0$\to$L1 jump exceeds its L1$\to$L2 jump by 0.2 pp); Boundary Delta ranges from 25.7 pp (PixArt-$\alpha$) to 30.3 pp (HiDream).
This pattern is not an artifact of the pipeline's auto-interpolated L1 anchors: computed on only the 7 categories whose L1 anchors were hand-authored (\SuppSec{\ref{app:taxonomy}}): $\Delta_B$ stays within 25.6--30.3 pp.

\subsection{Generative Model Analysis: Even the Safe Rung Is Not Safe}

\Tab{\ref{tab:t2i_models}} reports the per-category HGR matrix for the six models together with the SBS and $\Delta_B$ summaries.

\textbf{Reading note on SBS.} SBS captures how sharply failure concentrates at the boundary, not how safe a model is: the two highest-SBS models (HiDream 0.80, Janus-Pro 0.74) are also the two most harmful in absolute terms at L3, and all six models reach at least 47.5\% HGR@L3. Read SBS only alongside absolute HGR.

\textbf{Insight 2: Even the safe rung is not safe.}
\textit{All six models produce non-zero harm at L0 (0.5--3.5\%, of the same order as the evaluator's noise floor), and harm rates rise with generative capability; safety posture is baked into model weights through training and alignment data, not supplied by guard rails.}
PixArt-$\alpha$'s fine-tuning suppresses sexual HGR@L3 to 38.7\% versus 59.4\% for violence, but no model's training provides equivalent suppression for hate speech or misinformation; societal harms have been underrepresented in safety fine-tuning data. Across all six models, at least 47.5\% of L3 content remains harmful; zero-risk deployment needs output monitoring even for entirely safe prompts.

Construction--evaluation backbone overlap is audited in \SuppSec{\ref{app:backbone}}; no evaluated model shares an encoder or diffusion backbone with any construction component.

\subsection{Defense Mechanism Analysis: The L1 Gap Persists Under Protection}

\input{tables/defense_compact}

\Tab{\ref{tab:defense}} presents HGR reduction and SAS degradation for nine defense methods applied to SD1.4 across the NSFW, Legal, and Social groups; all nine target SD1.4's U-Net (DiT porting is future work).

\textbf{Insight 3: Defenses steepen the severity gradient.}
\textit{On our common SD1.4 backbone, all evaluated defenses achieve higher \emph{relative} suppression at L1 than at L3; yet absolute residual harm still peaks at L3, so the safety gap persists in absolute terms.}
Writing $\rho_\ell = (\mathrm{HGR}_{\mathrm{base}}(\ell) - \mathrm{HGR}_{\mathrm{def}}(\ell))/\mathrm{HGR}_{\mathrm{base}}(\ell)$ for a defense's relative HGR reduction at level $\ell$, all three paradigms suppress L1 more than L3 on NSFW content: inference-guided (NP, SLD, Safree) reaches $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{L1}} = 60.0\%$ vs.\ $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{L3}} = 34.6\%$, model-editing (UCE, SPEED) $49.7\%$ vs.\ $28.0\%$, fine-tuning (SafetyDPO, MACE, TRCE) $71.7\%$ vs.\ $50.0\%$: the strongest defenses nearly close the L1 gap on NSFW while L3 harm largely survives.
For example, MACE reduces NSFW L1 HGR from 26.9\% $\to$ 3.9\% ($\rho_{\mathrm{L1}} = 85.4\%$) and L3 from 74.5\% $\to$ 22.6\% ($\rho_{\mathrm{L3}} = 69.6\%$), raising the L3:L1 ratio from $2.8\times$ to $5.8\times$.
For Legal and Social categories, inference-guided methods stay weak at L3 ($\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{L3}} = 13.1\%$ and $21.9\%$) while still reducing L1 by 40--46\%; residual L3 HGR stays 46.6--55.4\%.
Critically, this finding requires \dataname's graded structure: a binary benchmark would report MACE as a strong defense ($\rho_{\mathrm{L3}} = 69\%$ on NSFW) while missing this skew entirely.

\textbf{Insight 4: The Utility--Safety Tradeoff Is Dominated by Two Methods.}
\textit{Under our joint $\Delta$HGR/SAS criterion, TRCE Pareto-dominates six of the seven remaining methods ($\Delta$HGR = 0.506 at an SAS cost of 0.008, versus e.g.\ SLD's 0.409 at a cost of 0.018); MACE occupies a distinct extreme point on the tradeoff curve.}
This audits the defense ecosystem behind Insights 1--3.
TRCE reduces HGR by $0.506$ at essentially no utility cost (SAS drop $-0.008$, below the next-cheapest defense's $0.014$), beating NP, SLD, Safree, UCE, SPEED, and SafetyDPO on both metrics at once (without discounting SLD's or SafetyDPO's documented competitiveness elsewhere).
RECE is excluded: it collapses on Legal and Social through concept diffuseness and semantic entanglement (\SuppSec{\ref{app:collapse}}; e.g., MACE's erasure of Public Figures degrades benign-portrait SAS by 40.1\%).
The remaining tradeoff is between TRCE and MACE: MACE erases more harm ($\Delta\mathrm{HGR} = 0.683$, $+35\%$ relative reduction) but pays $11\times$ the SAS penalty and $2.7\times$ the over-refusal rate (ORR 34.2\% vs.\ 12.8\%; \SuppSec{\ref{app:overrefusal}}).
Without MACE, defense strength and utility cost are uncorrelated ($r = 0.009$); including MACE, $r = 0.862$: the apparent general tradeoff is an artifact of its aggressive erasure.
The TRCE--SLD margin ($9.7$ pp) also far exceeds the worst-case Bernoulli SEM ($\lesssim 0.5$ pp), so the dominance is not a sampling artifact.

\subsection{Safety Filter Evaluation: Blind to the Boundary} \label{sec:filter}

\input{tables/filter}

\Tab{\ref{tab:filter}} reports FPR@L0, FBR, and severity sensitivity (L3--L0 detection gap) for five filters evaluated on \dataname reference images (a favorable setting, since reference images clearly manifest severity; the reported FBR values are thus a \emph{lower bound} on deployment bypass rates).

\textbf{Insight 5: Filter insensitivity to severity.}
\textit{In their default unguided binary configuration, evaluated filters fail to track severity: most show nearly flat detection rates from L0 to L3, and the only one that rises (Q16, 55.3\%$\to$67.2\%) does so while flagging over half of safe images.}
Q16, the strongest single filter, rises only from 55.3\% detection at L0 to 67.2\% at L3 (11.9 pp), while CLIP-NSFW misses 75\% of L3 content.
Q16 also flags 55.3\% of safe images as harmful (FPR@L0 = 0.553); Qwen2.5-VL nearly never false-alarms (FPR = 0.7\%) but misses 88\% of harmful content (FBR = 0.880); the purpose-built SD Filter rises only from 2.3\% to 7.8\% across rungs.
Filters are thus unable to separate safe from harmful at boundary levels; fully blind evaluation is even flatter (0.2\%/0.2\%/7.2\% at L1--L3; \SuppSec{\ref{app:metrics}}). LlavaGuard and Qwen2.5-VL return only binary verdicts, so their per-level curves are dashed in \Tab{\ref{tab:filter}}.

The gap is diagnosable, but is it closable? As a remediation check, we fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA on (image, risk explanation) $\rightarrow$ YES/NO pairs under a ladder-respecting 80/10/10 split (\SuppSec{\ref{app:remediation}}).
Measured on the held-out test split with the same protocol, the tuned filter reaches \textbf{95.2\%} detection at L1 with a \textbf{3.8\%} L0 false-positive rate, cutting the overall bypass rate from 0.880 (zero-shot backbone) to \textbf{0.022} (\Tab{\ref{tab:filter}}, bottom row).

\textbf{Insight 6: The safety gap is trainable-away.}
\textit{A filter fine-tuned on ladder data recovers the mid-severity band (FBR $0.880 \rightarrow 0.022$ benchmark-resident, $0.024$ fresh) while holding false alarms far below the strongest existing filter (FPR@L0 = 3.8\% vs.\ Q16's 55.3\%), converting \dataname from a diagnostic into a mitigation instrument.}
\textbf{Fresh-generation (deployment-regime) validation.} To test the tuned filter under realistic conditions, we regenerate every held-out test-split prompt with all six evaluated T2I models (\emph{not} construction models), using each model's default settings with checkers disabled and the ladder seeds (two images per prompt, 4,992 images in total).
Degenerate all-black generations are excluded from scoring.
On the remaining 4,626 images the tuned filter reaches 94.7\% L1, 99.0\% L2, and 99.0\% L3 detection (per-generator FBR 2.2--2.7\%) at a 4.0\% L0 false-positive rate; pooled FBR is 2.4\%, statistically indistinguishable from the 2.2\% reference-regime estimate, versus 65.3\% for the zero-shot backbone (\SuppTab{\ref{tab:filter_fresh}}).
Per-generator FBR varies only 2.2--2.7\% (L1 detection 94.2--95.2\%), and a Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone retrained under the identical protocol reaches test-split FBR of 0.6\% with 99.0\% L1 detection (pooled fresh-generation FBR 0.8\%); the tuned decision is also threshold-robust, with both error rates at or below 5\% across a wide operating band of the YES--NO margin threshold (\SuppSec{\ref{app:remediation}}).
The lower-bound caveat above therefore excludes the tuned row in both regimes.

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness: Attacks Exploit the Gap} \label{sec:attack}

\input{tables/attack_summary}

\Tab{\ref{tab:attacks}} presents ASR for six attack families against TRCE + LlavaGuard, the best utility-safety tradeoff configuration.
All exceed 55\% ASR: the newest, model-free PGJ\ \citep{huang2024pgj} leads at 72.4\%, followed by the multimodal MMA-Diffusion (68.7\%), query-based concept extraction (Ring-A-Bell, 65.6\%), and RL-driven prompt search (SneakyPrompt, 63.4\%), with the white-box gradient attacks UnlearnDiffAtk (61.5\%) and P4D (55.6\%) trailing; GenBreak-style red-team fine-tuning~\citep{wang2025genbreak} is out of scope.

\textbf{Insight 7: The safety gap is an active attack surface.}
\textit{A 55\% ASR floor across all six methods against the best-defended configuration, measured under our policy-guided evaluator (results would be lower under a stricter blind judge), confirms that the safety gap is not a passive evaluation artifact but a structurally exploitable channel.}
White-box gradient attacks trail the query-based and multimodal families (UnlearnDiffAtk 61.5\%, P4D 55.6\% vs.\ 63.4--68.7\%), because token optimization transfers least well to defended configurations.
The stronger attack families instead probe the text-filter $\to$ visual-output interface identified in Insight 5: prompts that evade text-level detection still escalate visually, and neither TRCE nor LlavaGuard closes this channel.
