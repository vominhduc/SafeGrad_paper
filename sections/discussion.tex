\section{Discussion}

\looseness=-1
\noindent\textbf{Calibration targets must move to the boundary.}
All evaluated mechanisms are calibrated on binary distinctions and steepen the severity gradient; sensitive deployments must evaluate L1--L2 explicitly, not trust L3-derived claims. Graded ladders also let each deployment choose its cut point (block from L1 for child-facing, from L2 for adults-only) and read off residual harm at that rung (\Tab{\ref{tab:t2i_models}}). The cross-modal handoff compounds this: text filters cannot decode visually adversarial prompts (stealthiness, \Sec{\ref{sec:quality}}), and image classifiers trained on explicit L3 content miss L1--L2 violations.

\noindent\textbf{From diagnosis to mitigation.}
\looseness=-1
Insight 6 turns the benchmark into a mitigation instrument: LoRA-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on ladders (\SuppSec{\ref{app:remediation}}) recovers the L1--L2 band zero-shot filters miss, transfers to fresh generations from all six models (pooled FBR 2.4\%), and is taxonomy-agnostic (regenerate ladders under one's own policy, retrain).
\red{Binary-tuned, description-only, image-mismatch, and description-free controls are future work; graded labels also invite severity-aware defenses: per-rung loss weighting for guidance, severity-stratified erasure for weight editing, graded preference pairs for safety tuning (\Tab{\ref{tab:defense}}).}

\looseness=-1
\noindent\textbf{Scope boundaries.}
\red{Copyright and PID show lower HGR than NSFW (\Tab{\ref{tab:t2i_models}}): MLLM classifiers are weaker at identity harms, so the gap there is likely \emph{underestimated}; severity definitions are Western (US/EU) policy-grounded (non-Western probe: \SuppSec{\ref{app:cultural}}).}
