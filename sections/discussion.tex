\paragraph{Rethinking the safety calibration target.}
All evaluated mechanisms are calibrated on binary distinctions, concentrating protection at L3 while leaving the L1--L2 regime underserved; defenses trained this way steepen the severity gradient rather than closing it. Deployments in sensitive contexts must explicitly evaluate the L1--L2 regime rather than trusting L3-derived safety claims. Severity-aware defense (optimizing explicitly for L1 and L2 suppression using ASL-generated training data) is the most direct path to closing the boundary gap; the released pipeline supports this direction.

\paragraph{The cross-modal gap as a root cause.}
Text filters cannot decode visually adversarial prompts, and image classifiers trained on explicit L3 content fail at L1--L2 violations: the \red{72.32\%} stealthy attack rate at L3 shows this gap is by adversarial design. Future systems require multimodal classifiers trained jointly on text and image signals across the full severity spectrum, and severity-aware defenses that explicitly optimize for L1--L2 suppression rather than L3 alone.

\paragraph{Category-specific implications.}
Copyright and PID categories exhibit structurally lower HGR than NSFW categories (\Tab{\ref{tab:t2i_models}}), not because the models are safer on these topics but because current MLLM classifiers are weaker at evaluating fine-grained identity harms (e.g., character likeness). 
This points to a need for specialized visual recognizers (e.g., character-similarity models for Copyright and document-detection models for PID) as a complement to general-purpose MLLM evaluators. The safety gap in these categories is likely \emph{underestimated} by the current benchmark.

\red{\paragraph{Cross-cultural scope.}
Our severity definitions reflect a Western (US/EU) legal and regulatory framework, a property inherited from the safety-alignment data underlying current LLMs, MLLMs, and T2I models rather than from the benchmark design. Our cultural nuance analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:cultural}) shows that non-Western entities are less protected: they incur $13.4$ pp higher L3 HGR for Public Figures (64.9\% $\rightarrow$ 78.3\%) and $11.2$ pp higher for Copyright (72.9\% $\rightarrow$ 84.1\%) than their Western counterparts, likely because non-Western public figures and IP are underrepresented in safety fine-tuning data. Since jurisdictions differ in what they regulate, we treat this as an explicit scope limitation and establish it as the foundation for a multilingual, multi-jurisdictional extension of \dataname. The ASL pipeline directly supports re-instantiating the taxonomy under alternative legal frameworks and non-English prompt construction.}

\red{\paragraph{Toward finer severity resolution.}
Several L2$\to$L3 transitions (e.g., from ``nudity behind objects'' to pornography) are intentionally drastic, as they map onto the operational thresholds of major commercial platform policies; for research purposes this resolution can be coarse. We plan to incorporate finer severity gradations in the next version of the public dataset release to close these residual harm-coverage gaps.}
