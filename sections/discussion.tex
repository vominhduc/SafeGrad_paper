\section{Discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Calibration targets must move to the boundary.}
All evaluated mechanisms are calibrated on binary distinctions and steepen the severity gradient; sensitive deployments must evaluate L1--L2 explicitly rather than trust L3-derived claims. The cross-modal handoff makes this worse: text filters cannot decode visually adversarial prompts (the stealthiness analysis in \Sec{\ref{sec:quality}}), and image classifiers trained on explicit L3 content miss L1--L2 violations.

\noindent\textbf{From diagnosis to mitigation.}
Insight 6 turns the benchmark from a measurement tool into an instrument: LoRA-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on \dataname's ladders (\SuppSec{\ref{app:remediation}}) recovers the L1--L2 band zero-shot filters miss, transfers to fresh generations from all six models (pooled FBR 2.4\%, prompt-aware), and is taxonomy-agnostic (regenerate ladders under one's own policy, retrain).
\red{A binary-tuned control (ladder-structure causality), description-only and image-mismatch controls (visual grounding), and description-free operation are future work; the graded labels likewise invite severity-aware defenses (per-rung loss weighting for guidance, severity-stratified erasure for weight editing, graded preference pairs for safety tuning; \Tab{\ref{tab:defense}}).}

\noindent\textbf{Scope boundaries.}
Copyright and PID show structurally lower HGR than NSFW categories (\Tab{\ref{tab:t2i_models}}): MLLM classifiers are weaker at fine-grained identity harms, so the gap there is likely \emph{underestimated}.
Severity definitions are Western (US/EU) policy-grounded; a bounded non-Western probe (\SuppSec{\ref{app:cultural}}) quantifies the asymmetry, and jurisdiction-calibrated anchors are future work.
