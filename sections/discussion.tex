\section{Discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Calibration targets must move to the boundary.}
All evaluated mechanisms are calibrated on binary distinctions and steepen the severity gradient, so sensitive deployments must evaluate L1--L2 explicitly rather than trust claims derived from L3. The text-to-image handoff compounds this: text filters miss visually adversarial prompts (\Sec{\ref{sec:quality}}), and image classifiers trained on explicit L3 content miss L1--L2 violations.

\noindent\textbf{From diagnosis to mitigation.}
\looseness=-1
Insight~6 turns the benchmark into a training resource, and the recipe is taxonomy-agnostic: regenerate ladders under another policy and retrain.
Binary-tuned, description-only, and image-mismatch controls remain future work.
The graded labels also invite severity-aware defenses beyond L3 suppression (\Tab{\ref{tab:defense}}): per-rung loss weighting for inference-time guidance, severity-stratified erasure for weight editing, and graded preference pairs for safety fine-tuning.

\noindent\textbf{Protocol-relative findings.}
Orderings, transition shapes, and the L1$\to$L2 peak (Insights~1--3) compare conditions under one protocol. Absolute HGR and ASR depend on the evaluator (at L3, 76.4\% guided vs.\ 7.2\% blind), so they rank methods rather than measure deployment risk.
