\section{Adversarial Severity Ladders Pipeline} \label{sec:pipeline}

\subsection{Pipeline Overview}
The Adversarial Severity Ladders (ASL) pipeline is automated but works under human oversight: the authors write and review the taxonomy, severity definitions, and escalation rules. Compared with fully human annotation, this has four advantages: (1) scale without annotator self-selection bias; (2) programmatic severity calibration through escalation rules, which avoids inter-rater inconsistency on fine-grained severity judgments~\citep{aroyo2015truth}; (3) deterministic reproducibility given fixed seeds and checkpoints; and (4) no large-scale harmful-content exposure for workers, which carries documented psychological risks~\citep{steiger2021psychological}.
The pipeline has four stages.

\noindent\textbf{Stage 1: Safe Prompt Generation.}
For each category, we generate seed safe prompts using Mistral-7B-Instruct~\citep{Mistral} (36.5\%), Llama-2-7b-chat-hf~\citep{llama2} (31.6\%), Dolphin-2.9-Llama3-8B~\citep{hartford2024dolphin} (23.4\%), and Vicuna-7B~\citep{vicuna} (8.6\%; shares rounded), spanning the safety-alignment spectrum to maximize stylistic diversity.
Near-duplicate prompts are removed via FAISS clustering on \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} embeddings (cosine threshold $\geq 0.95$), retaining one representative per cluster.

\noindent\textbf{Stage 2: Risky Prompt Synthesis.}
Llama-3-70B-Instruct~\citep{llama3herdofmodels} escalates each seed through four severity rungs by modulating risk dimensions (subject, action, context, intensity) per human-authored category-specific escalation rules.
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct~\citep{qwen2.5technicalreport} scores each ladder; any adjacent gap below $0.4$ in the normalized 0--3 risk score triggers regeneration.

\noindent\textbf{Stage 3: Reference Image Generation.}
Images are generated using SDXL~\citep{podell2023sdxl} (55.8\%), Z-Turbo~\citep{zimage2025efficient} (26.8\%), FLUX.1-dev~\citep{labs2025flux1} (16.4\%), and SD3.5-Large~\citep{sd35large} (1.1\%) with safety checkers disabled.
The same model and seed are used for all four rungs within a ladder, isolating severity differences from generative variance.

\noindent\textbf{Stage 4: Verification.}
Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking~\citep{qwen3vl2025} verifies each prompt-image pair for content alignment and severity match (tolerance $\leq 1$ rung) and generates per-image risk explanations; failing pairs are regenerated.
Verification is per-rung and description-guided (unlike the unguided filters of Insight~5), and cross-rung continuity is audited separately (\SuppSec{\ref{app:construction:continuity}}).
Incomplete ladders are excluded, yielding 1,026 validated ladders (${\sim}32$ A100 GPU-hours in total).

\subsection{Dataset Quality Analysis} \label{sec:quality}
\Tab{\ref{tab:quality_full}} reports quality metrics across all 11 categories.
\textbf{Severity monotonicity.} Spearman $\rho = 0.962$ between rung index and verifier risk score measures the internal consistency of the pipeline's rubric (Stage~4 verifies images to within $\pm 1$ rung of their label). Human grounding of the rung labels comes separately from the blinded validation study ($\kappa = 0.73$; \SuppSec{\ref{app:human}}).
\textbf{Adversarial stealthiness.} The mean stealthy rate is 72.32\%: most L3 prompts contain no explicit harmful keyword, so keyword-based text filters miss them.
\textbf{Prompt fluency.} Mean PPL@L3 is $33.92$, lower than T2I-RiskyPrompt's $86$, which further undermines perplexity-based detection.
\textbf{Semantic escalation magnitude.} The mean angular distance from L0 to L3 is $\Delta\theta = 40.23\degree$.
\textbf{Independent relabeling.} Claude-3.7-Sonnet re-labels 100 ladders (400 pairs), and the automated evaluator agrees on 96\% (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.76$).
\textbf{Lexical diversity.} Across all 4,104 prompts, text is highly varied between ladders (Self-BLEU-4 $= 0.05$, distinct-2 $= 0.89$), which rules out templated generation. Within a ladder, where thematic overlap is expected, Self-BLEU-4 stays low at $0.14$ (distinct-2 $= 0.76$).
