\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Evaluated Methods}

\textbf{T2I Models.} To ensure evaluation fairness, we exclude the four construction models
and instead evaluate six architecturally independent open-source models: SD1.4, SD2.1~\citep{rombach2022ldm}, PixArt-$\alpha$~\citep{chen2023pixart}, CogView4~\citep{thudm2025cogview4}, Janus-Pro~\citep{chen2025janusPro}, and HiDream~\citep{cai2025hidream}.

\noindent
\textbf{Defense Methods.} We implement nine methods across three paradigms: \textit{inference-guided} (NP, SLD~\citep{schramowski2023safe}, Safree~\citep{yoon2024safree}), \textit{model-editing} (UCE~\citep{gandikota2024unified}, RECE~\citep{gong2024rece}, SPEED~\citep{li2026speed}), and \textit{fine-tuning} (SafetyDPO~\citep{liu2024safetydpo}, MACE~\citep{lu2024mace}, TRCE~\citep{chen2025trce}), all applied to SD1.4: six of the nine are U-Net-specific weight editors without published DiT implementations and SD1.4 is the concept-erasure literature's standard backbone.

\noindent
\textbf{Safety Filters.} We evaluate Q16~\citep{schramowski2022q16}, CLIP-NSFW~\citep{laionAI}, SD Filter~\citep{rombach2022ldm}, LlavaGuard~\citep{helff2024llavaguard}, and Qwen2.5-VL~\citep{qwen2.5technicalreport}.

\noindent
\textbf{Attack Strategies.} We evaluate SneakyPrompt~\citep{yang2023sneakyprompt}, Ring-A-Bell~\citep{tsai2024ringabell}, UnlearnDiffAtk~\citep{zhang2024unlearndiffattk}, MMA-Diffusion~\citep{yang2024mma}, and P4D~\citep{chin2024p4d} against the best-defended configuration.
We use only L2 and L3 prompts, as lower-severity prompts rarely produce harmful outputs even under attack.
All attacks follow their publicly released reference implementations without further tuning, and ASR in \Tab{\ref{tab:attacks}} is computed over the same 2,052-prompt set ($2 \times 1,026$ L2--L3 prompts) for every attack, so rows are directly comparable.

\noindent\textbf{Generation protocol.}
For prompt-conditioned evaluation (models, defenses, attacks), we generate two images per prompt per model using default inference settings \emph{with safety checkers disabled} (filters instead classify static reference images; the tuned filter is additionally validated on fresh generations, \Sec{\ref{sec:filter}}), a deliberate choice that measures each model's raw generative risk independent of its guard layers.
HGR is computed at the image level, as the fraction of generated images flagged by the evaluator ensemble.
All conclusions rest on cross-method comparisons under this identical protocol.
With native checkers \emph{activated} (SD1.4, HiDream), L3 HGR falls to 47.3\%/63.4\% while L1/L2 HGR drop by at most 3.6\%, leaving the L1--L2 boundary unaddressed.

\noindent\textbf{Automated evaluator.}
Since no existing safety filter covers all 11 risk categories, we employ an ensemble of three MLLMs \emph{excluded from dataset construction} (InternVL2-8B~\citep{chen2024internvl}, Phi-4-multimodal-instruct~\citep{phi4multimodal2025}, and Gemma-3-4b-it~\citep{gemma_3_2025}) as the automated evaluator $\phi$.
Each model receives the sample-specific risk description derived from the escalation rules; $\phi(I) = 1$ if at least two of the three models flag the image.
On a stratified held-out subset of 200 pairs (disjoint from the construction validation set) re-labeled by Claude-3.7-Sonnet as an independent verifier, the ensemble achieves 91\% average label agreement across all 11 categories (vs.\ 59.8\% for Q16), supporting the reliability of the automated evaluator.
Providing the risk description and intended severity to the evaluator is deliberate: it models a deployed severity-aware filter with policy access rather than a naive classifier. A blinded ablation stripping the intended-level label shifts HGR by under 2.4\% on macro average, and a fully-blind ensemble still detects only 0.2\%/0.2\%/7.2\% at L1--L3 (guided: 19.7\%/59.0\%/76.4\%); guidance raises sensitivity but does not create the gap (\SuppSec{\ref{app:metrics}}).
To ground the evaluator in human judgment, we conduct a blinded human study on 500 stratified prompt--image pairs judged by three independent annotators, with 60\% of the budget at the decision-critical boundary (150 pairs each at L1 and L2); consensus agrees with the pipeline's labels at $\kappa = 0.73$ (substantial; protocol in \SuppSec{\ref{app:human}}), confirming the ensemble is a reliable proxy for human judgment on this task.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We use five core metrics, plus a derived boundary statistic:
\textbf{HGR} (harmful generation rate), \textbf{SBS} (safety boundary slope), \textbf{SAS} (CLIP semantic alignment), \textbf{FBR} (filter bypass rate), \textbf{ASR} (attack success rate), plus the per-model Boundary Delta $\boldsymbol{\Delta_B}$ (the L1$\to$L2 HGR jump) (\SuppSec{\ref{app:metrics}}); within-model Bernoulli SEs stay $\leq 1.1$ pp, and aggregated figures report $\pm 1$ SEM (standard error of the mean) across the six models.
