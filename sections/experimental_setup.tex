\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Evaluated Methods}

\textbf{T2I Models.} To ensure evaluation fairness, we exclude the four construction models
and instead evaluate six architecturally independent open-source models: SD1.4, SD2.1~\citep{rombach2022ldm}, PixArt-$\alpha$~\citep{chen2023pixart}, CogView4~\citep{thudm2025cogview4}, Janus-Pro~\citep{chen2025janusPro}, and HiDream~\citep{cai2025hidream}.

\noindent
\textbf{Defense Methods.} We implement nine methods across three paradigms: \textit{inference-guided} (NP, SLD~\citep{schramowski2023safe}, Safree~\citep{yoon2024safree}), \textit{model-editing} (UCE~\citep{gandikota2024unified}, RECE~\citep{gong2024rece}, SPEED~\citep{li2026speed}), and \textit{fine-tuning} (SafetyDPO~\citep{liu2024safetydpo}, MACE~\citep{lu2024mace}, TRCE~\citep{chen2025trce}), all applied to SD1.4: six of the nine are U-Net-specific weight editors without published DiT implementations and SD1.4 is the concept-erasure literature's standard backbone.

\noindent
\textbf{Safety Filters.} We evaluate Q16~\citep{schramowski2022q16}, CLIP-NSFW~\citep{laionAI}, SD Filter~\citep{rombach2022ldm}, LlavaGuard~\citep{helff2024llavaguard}, Qwen2.5-VL~\citep{qwen2.5technicalreport}, and InternVL2-8B~\citep{chen2024internvl}.

\noindent
\textbf{Attack Strategies.} We evaluate six attack families---SneakyPrompt~\citep{yang2023sneakyprompt}, Ring-A-Bell~\citep{tsai2024ringabell}, UnlearnDiffAtk~\citep{zhang2024unlearndiffattk}, MMA-Diffusion~\citep{yang2024mma}, P4D~\citep{chin2024p4d}, and the recent perception-guided jailbreak (PGJ)~\citep{huang2024pgj}, the most recent protocol-compatible attack---against the strongest utility-preserving defended configuration we evaluate (Sec.~\ref{sec:attack}).
We use only L2 and L3 prompts, as lower-severity prompts rarely produce harmful outputs even under attack.
All attacks use their public reference implementations without tuning; ASR (\Tab{\ref{tab:attacks}}) is computed over the same 2,052 L2--L3 prompts for every attack, so rows are directly comparable.

\noindent\textbf{Generation protocol.}
For prompt-conditioned evaluation (models, defenses, attacks), we generate two images per prompt per model using default inference settings \emph{with safety checkers disabled} (filters instead classify static reference images; the tuned filter is additionally validated on fresh generations, \Sec{\ref{sec:filter}}), measuring each model's raw generative risk independent of its guard layers.
HGR is computed at the image level, as the fraction of generated images flagged by the evaluator ensemble.
All conclusions rest on cross-method comparisons under this identical protocol.
As a deployment-layer control (checker \emph{activated} on SD1.4 and HiDream), L3 HGR falls to 47.3\%/63.4\% while L1/L2 HGR drop by at most 3.6\%, leaving the L1--L2 boundary unaddressed.

\noindent\textbf{Automated evaluator.}
Since no existing safety filter covers all 11 risk categories, we employ an ensemble of three MLLMs \emph{excluded from dataset construction} (InternVL2-8B~\citep{chen2024internvl}, Phi-4-multimodal-instruct~\citep{phi4multimodal2025}, and Gemma-3-4b-it~\citep{gemma_3_2025}) as the automated evaluator $\phi$.
Each model receives the sample-specific risk description derived from the escalation rules, matching the information budget of a deployed severity-aware filter with policy access; $\phi(I) = 1$ if at least two of the three models flag the image.
We validate the ensemble at three levels.
(i) an independent Claude-3.7-Sonnet relabeling of 200 held-out stratified pairs agrees on 91\% (vs.\ 59.8\% for Q16);
(ii) stripping the intended-level label shifts HGR by under 2.4\% on macro average, and a fully blind ensemble detects almost nothing below L3 (\SuppSec{\ref{app:metrics}}), so guidance raises sensitivity but does not create the gap;
(iii) a blinded human study on 500 stratified pairs (three independent annotators, 60\% of the budget at the L1--L2 boundary) agrees with the pipeline's labels at $\kappa = 0.73$ (\SuppSec{\ref{app:human}}).
All absolute rates in Secs.~\ref{sec:gap}--\ref{sec:attack} are protocol-relative (cross-method comparisons under one identical protocol); since (iii) is rule-instructed, a fully blind human harm rating remains future work.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We use five core metrics, plus a derived boundary statistic:
\textbf{HGR} (harmful generation rate), \textbf{SBS} (safety boundary sharpness), \textbf{SAS} (CLIP semantic alignment), \textbf{FBR} (filter bypass rate), \textbf{ASR} (attack success rate), plus the per-model Boundary Delta $\boldsymbol{\Delta_B}$ (the L1$\to$L2 HGR jump) (\SuppSec{\ref{app:metrics}}); within-model Bernoulli SEs stay $\leq 1.1$ pp for category-pooled HGR ($n = 2{,}052$ images; per-category bounds are correspondingly larger), and aggregated figures report $\pm 1$ SEM (standard error of the mean) across the six models.
