\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Evaluated Methods}

\textbf{T2I Models.} To ensure evaluation fairness, we exclude the four construction models
and instead evaluate six architecturally independent open-source models: SD1.4, SD2.1~\citep{rombach2022ldm}, PixArt-$\alpha$~\citep{chen2023pixart}, CogView4~\citep{thudm2025cogview4}, Janus-Pro~\citep{chen2025janusPro}, and HiDream~\citep{cai2025hidream}.

\noindent
\textbf{Defense Methods.} We implement nine methods across three paradigms: \textit{inference-guided} (NP (negative prompt), SLD~\citep{schramowski2023safe}, Safree~\citep{yoon2024safree}), \textit{model-editing} (UCE~\citep{gandikota2024unified}, RECE~\citep{gong2024rece}, SPEED~\citep{li2026speed}), and \textit{fine-tuning} (SafetyDPO~\citep{liu2024safetydpo}, MACE~\citep{lu2024mace}, TRCE~\citep{chen2025trce}), all applied to SD1.4 as the base model.
SD1.4 is selected as the defense backbone because six of the nine defenses are U-Net-specific weight editors with no published DiT implementation, and SD1.4 is the standard reference backbone in the concept-erasure literature; since filter evaluation uses static reference images and attack scores are averaged, these findings should transfer across backbones.

\noindent
\textbf{Safety Filters.} We evaluate Q16~\citep{schramowski2022q16}, CLIP-NSFW~\citep{laionAI}, SD Filter~\citep{rombach2022ldm}, LlavaGuard~\citep{helff2024llavaguard}, and Qwen2.5-VL~\citep{qwen2.5technicalreport}.

\noindent
\textbf{Attack Strategies.} We evaluate SneakyPrompt~\citep{yang2023sneakyprompt}, Ring-A-Bell~\citep{tsai2024ringabell}, UnlearnDiffAtk~\citep{zhang2024unlearndiffattk}, MMA-Diffusion~\citep{yang2024mma}, and P4D~\citep{chin2024p4d} against the best-defended model-filter configurations.
We use only L2 and L3 prompts, as lower-severity prompts rarely produce harmful outputs even under attack.

\noindent\textbf{Generation protocol.}
We generate two images per prompt per model using default inference settings \emph{with safety checkers disabled}, a deliberate choice that measures the raw generative risk of each model's diffusion process independently of its guard layers.
A prompt is harmful if at least one of the two images is flagged by our evaluator ensemble.
This per-prompt OR protocol models adversarial regeneration; all conclusions rest on cross-method comparisons under the identical protocol.
With native checkers \emph{activated} on two representative models (SD1.4, HiDream), explicit L3 HGR falls to 47.3\%/63.4\% but L1/L2 HGR drop by at most 3.6\%: checkers act as threshold switches for explicit content and leave the L1--L2 boundary unaddressed.

\noindent\textbf{Automated evaluator.}
Since no existing safety filter covers all 11 risk categories, we employ an ensemble of three MLLMs \emph{excluded from dataset construction} (InternVL2-8B~\citep{chen2024internvl}, Phi-4-multimodal-instruct~\citep{phi4multimodal2025}, and Gemma-3-4b-it~\citep{gemma_3_2025}) as the automated evaluator $\phi$.
Each model receives the sample-specific risk description derived from the escalation rules; $\phi(I) = 1$ if at least two of the three models flag the image.
On a stratified held-out subset of 200 pairs (disjoint from the construction validation set) re-labeled by Claude-3.7-Sonnet as an independent verifier, the ensemble achieves 91\% average label agreement across all 11 categories (vs.\ 59.8\% for Q16), supporting the reliability of the automated evaluator.
Providing the risk description and intended severity to the evaluator is deliberate: it models a deployed severity-aware filter with policy access rather than a naive classifier. A blinded ablation stripping the intended-level label shifts HGR by under 2.4\% on macro average, indicating judgments driven by visual content rather than prompt cues.
To ground the evaluator in human judgment, we conduct a blinded human study on 500 stratified prompt--image pairs judged by three independent annotators, with 60\% of the budget at the decision-critical boundary (150 pairs each at L1 and L2); consensus agrees with the pipeline's labels at $\kappa = 0.73$ (substantial; protocol in \SuppSec{\ref{app:human}}), confirming the ensemble is a reliable proxy for human judgment on this task.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We use five metrics that jointly characterize safety and utility across the severity spectrum:
\textbf{HGR} (harmful generation rate per severity level), \textbf{SBS} (normalized safety boundary slope), \textbf{SAS} (CLIP-based semantic alignment), \textbf{FBR} (filter bypass rate), and \textbf{ASR} (attack success rate) (\SuppSec{\ref{app:metrics}}); within-model Bernoulli SEs on HGR stay $\leq 1.1$ pp, and aggregated figures report $\pm 1$ SEM across the six models.
